It's the last week of term. Scores are in, the broadsheet is done — and now there are forty report cards waiting for a **class teacher's remark**, and another four hundred for the **principal's comment**.

By card number thirty, every child is "a good student who should work harder."

Parents notice. A report card comment is often the only sentence on the whole card that is written *about their child* rather than about a score. It is worth getting right.

This guide gives you **over 150 ready-to-use report card comments** for Nigerian primary and secondary schools, organised by performance, behaviour, attendance, subject and promotion — plus a simple formula for writing your own.

## The Formula for a Good Report Card Comment

Every useful comment has three parts:

1. **A specific strength** — something the child genuinely does well.
2. **A specific area to improve** — one or two things, not a list of faults.
3. **A next step** — what the child or parent can do next term.

> **Strength + Area to improve + Next step**

Compare:

- ❌ "Fair result. Can do better."
- ✅ "Amara is attentive in class and her handwriting has improved. She needs more practice with fractions; working through ten sums at home each week will help."

The second takes ten more seconds to write and tells the parent exactly what to do.

A few rules that apply to every comment below:

- **Use the child's name** where the card allows it.
- **Describe the work, not the child.** "Her essays lack detail" — not "She is lazy."
- **Be honest.** A glowing comment on a failing card destroys trust when the parent sees the scores.
- **Keep it short.** Two sentences is plenty for a class teacher; one for a principal.

## Words to Avoid on a Report Card

Some words do more harm than good, and parents remember them for years:

| Avoid | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Lazy | Needs to put in more consistent effort |
| Dull / slow | Is still developing in… / needs more time and practice with… |
| Stubborn | Should learn to accept correction |
| Troublesome | Needs to follow class rules more closely |
| Always / never | Often / sometimes |
| Cannot | Is learning to |

The goal is not to soften the truth — it is to describe behaviour the child can change, rather than label a child who cannot.

## How to Write 40 Comments Without Repeating Yourself

- **Write the remark straight after you check the scores**, while the child's term is fresh in your mind — not all at the end.
- **Keep a short note per child during the term** — one line when something notable happens. Those notes become your comments.
- **Group by pattern, then personalise.** Start from a comment above that matches the pattern, then add one detail only that child has.
- **Let your software do the arithmetic.** When totals, averages and positions are calculated automatically, the hours you used to spend checking sums can go into comments instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a teacher write as a remark on a report card?

A good report card remark names one specific strength, one specific area to improve and a next step, in a sentence or two. For example: 'Tobi reads fluently and contributes well in class. He should practise his times tables at home to improve in Mathematics.'

### What is a good comment for an excellent student?

Be specific about what made the term excellent rather than writing 'Excellent result' alone. For example: 'An outstanding term. Her consistent effort and careful work show in every subject. Keep aiming high.'

### How do you comment on a weak student's report card without discouraging them?

Start with something genuine the child does well, name the one or two subjects that need attention, and give a concrete action for the home. For example: 'Chidi is cheerful and well-behaved. He needs extra practice in reading and Mathematics; 20 minutes of reading at home each evening will help.'

### What is the difference between the class teacher's remark and the principal's comment?

The class teacher's remark is detailed and personal because the teacher sees the child daily. The principal's or head teacher's comment is shorter and usually confirms the overall performance, the promotion decision or a call to action for the parent.
