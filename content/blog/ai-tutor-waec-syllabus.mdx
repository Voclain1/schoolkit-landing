If you've searched for "AI tutor" for your child, you've probably found a dozen apps
that all promise the same thing: personalized learning, instant answers, 24/7 help.
Almost none of them mention WAEC by name. That's not an accident — most of these
tools are built on general-purpose AI models trained on whatever the internet happens
to contain, not on the specific syllabus a Nigerian student is actually examined on.

## Why "generic AI" and "WAEC-ready" aren't the same thing

A general AI model can explain photosynthesis. It can also explain photosynthesis in
a way that has nothing to do with how WAEC phrases the question, what depth of answer
earns full marks, or which past-question patterns show up year after year. For a
parent trying to judge whether a tool is worth paying for, this is the single most
important distinction — and the one most marketing pages gloss over.

An AI tutor grounded in the WAEC syllabus works differently. It's built to know:

- What topics are actually examinable at each level, not just what's academically interesting
- How WAEC structures its questions, so practice feels like the real exam instead of a generic quiz
- Where students typically lose marks — vague definitions, missing steps in workings, incomplete diagrams — and correct for those specific habits

## What to check before trusting any AI tutor

If you're evaluating options for your child or your school, a few questions cut
through the marketing quickly:

1. **Ask what it's grounded in.** "Trained on educational content" is vague. "Aligned to the WAEC syllabus" is specific and checkable.
2. **Ask how it handles past questions.** A tool that references actual WAEC-style questions is doing something different from one that generates generic practice problems.
3. **Ask what happens when a student gets something wrong.** Good WAEC-aligned tutoring explains *why* an answer loses marks under WAEC's actual grading style, not just whether it's right or wrong in the abstract.

## Why this matters more in Nigeria than the marketing suggests

Most ed-tech AI tools are built for a US or UK curriculum first, then loosely adapted.
For a Nigerian student preparing for WAEC, that gap shows up exactly where it matters
most — in exam technique, mark-scheme awareness, and topic coverage that matches what
will actually be tested. A tutor that's WAEC-aligned from the ground up isn't a nice-to-have
feature. It's the difference between a study tool and a distraction with a good UI.
