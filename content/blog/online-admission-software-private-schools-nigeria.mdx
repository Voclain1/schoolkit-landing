A parent fills in your school’s admission form on Monday.

By Friday, she has sent the child’s birth certificate to the secretary on WhatsApp, the previous report card to the headteacher and a follow-up message to the proprietor.

Everyone has part of the application. Nobody has the whole file.

An online form can collect information. **An admission system needs to help your staff finish the admission.**

If you are comparing online admission software for a Nigerian private school, test the journey from the parent’s first application to the child appearing on the correct class list.

## What Are You Actually Buying?

A useful admission workflow has several stages:

**Application → documents → staff review → entrance assessment → decision → offer → acceptance → student record.**

Some schools skip the entrance assessment. Others interview the parent or request a transfer document. The software should fit your process without encouraging staff to keep the real status somewhere else.

Ask the vendor to demonstrate your stages, using a sample applicant.

## When Is a Google Form Enough?

For a school receiving a few applications a month, a form and a carefully managed spreadsheet may work.

The trouble starts when your team needs to answer questions such as:

- Which applicants have not uploaded their documents?
- Who has completed an entrance assessment?
- Which families received an offer?
- Who accepted but has not completed the next step?
- Has this child already been added to the student register?

If someone checks three tools to answer each question, you have found the reason to consider dedicated software.

| Requirement | Basic form and spreadsheet | Connected admissions system |
|---|---|---|
| Collect parent and child details | Usually straightforward | Should be straightforward |
| Collect supporting documents | Depends on form settings and access | Ask for a mobile upload demonstration |
| Track review and decisions | Usually maintained manually | Should remain attached to the application |
| Follow an application’s history | Often spread across messages and sheets | Ask to see who changed the status and when |
| Create the student record | Often copied or imported | Test the accepted-application handover |

Neither option removes the need for staff to make a careful admission decision.

## Test the Form on a Parent’s Phone

Open the application page on an ordinary phone, not just the vendor’s laptop.

Try completing it with one hand and an intermittent connection.

Can a parent understand the requested class and academic session? Are required fields clearly marked? Can they upload a readable photo of a document without needing a scanner?

Also ask what happens if they close the page halfway through. Do they lose everything? Can they resume? Do they receive a reference they can use when contacting the school?

These are questions to demonstrate, not features to assume.

A long form can look thorough while making it harder for a family to finish applying. Collect what the school needs for the decision, and explain why sensitive documents are required.

## Keep Documents With the Right Applicant

Two children can share a surname. Siblings can apply for different classes. One parent may submit an application twice because the first attempt appeared to fail.

Ask how the system identifies duplicates and links each document to the correct application.

Your staff should be able to see what is missing without downloading every attachment into a general folder.

Use fictional records during the demo. There is no need to share real children’s documents simply to evaluate software.

## Make the Decision Process Visible

Consider this **illustrative application**:

| Stage | Record staff need |
|---|---|
| Applied for Basic 3 | Child’s details, guardian contact and requested session |
| Documents checked | Which documents were reviewed and what is missing |
| Assessment completed | Assessment date, result and reviewer’s notes |
| Offer sent | Offered class and any conditions |
| Family accepted | Acceptance status and agreed next step |
| Admitted | Student identifier and class assignment |

The point is not to add more administrative steps. It is to stop the school asking the same family for information it already has.

Ask whether each staff member sees only the applications and details their role requires.

## The Most Important Demo: Accept the Offer

Tell the vendor:

> Please admit this applicant and show us the student record, guardian link and class assignment that result.

Check the child’s name, date of birth, class and guardian contact.

Then ask what happens if the parent corrects a spelling before admission. Does the correction carry through, or will staff need to fix two separate records?

If your accounts officer needs an admission charge or first-term bill, ask how that step connects. An application form is not proof that billing is integrated.

## If You Charge an Application Fee, Test It Separately

Do not assume that “online admissions” includes every type of payment.

Ask the provider to show:

1. How the applicant knows the amount due.
2. How the payment is linked to the application.
3. What staff see when a payment is pending or fails.
4. How duplicate payments are identified.
5. Who handles refunds and how they are recorded.

Have the provider confirm gateway charges and responsibility for support. Your school’s admission policy should also explain whether the fee is refundable.

## Ask About Access, Retention and Export

Admission files contain children’s information and family contact details.

Before subscribing, ask who can view, download and change those records. Find out how access is removed when a staff member leaves.

Agree how long unsuccessful applications need to remain in the system and how the school handles deletion requests under its policy and applicable requirements.

Finally, ask for a sample export. You should know what happens to your applications if you change providers.

A sensible answer names the records, format and process. “Your data is safe” does not answer the export question.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should school admission software include?

Look for forms, document uploads, application status, review, assessment records, offers and a reliable handover into student records.

### Is a Google Form enough?

It can be for a small number of applications. Consider dedicated software when status tracking and copying information between tools become regular work.

### Does SchoolKit support online admissions?

Yes. Confirm the plan and ask to see your school’s process demonstrated.

### Must the software collect application fees?

Only if your school charges them. Payment handling needs its own demonstration and written confirmation of costs.
