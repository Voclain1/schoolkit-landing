The teacher opens the attendance register at 8:05 a.m.

The internet stops working at 8:07.

Can she finish marking the class? Will the register survive a page refresh? And when the connection returns, how does she know the entries reached the school’s central record?

Those are better questions than simply asking, **“Does it work offline?”**

If you are choosing offline school management software in Nigeria, ask the vendor to turn off the connection during the demo. Then test the work your staff actually need to do.

## “Offline” Can Mean Different Things

A product may use the word to describe very different arrangements.

| What the vendor means | What to ask |
|---|---|
| A desktop program installed on one computer | How are records backed up and shared with other staff? |
| A web app that opens previously loaded pages | Can staff make changes, or only read cached information? |
| Selected tasks queued for later syncing | Which tasks, which devices and how are sync failures shown? |
| A local school server | What equipment, maintenance and backup arrangements are required? |

None of these answers is automatically wrong.

What matters is whether the arrangement fits your school. A bursar working on one office computer and teachers taking attendance on separate phones have different needs.

## Make a List of Tasks Before Comparing Products

Write down what must continue during a connection problem.

For example:

- view a class list;
- mark attendance;
- enter scores;
- look up a student’s previously loaded information;
- prepare work for later syncing.

Then separate those tasks from actions that depend on another online service.

A new gateway payment needs a connection to the payment provider. Sending a message needs the relevant delivery service. Sharing a changed record with another device requires a way for the information to reach it.

A queued action can be useful. It should still be clearly labelled as pending until it has completed.

## Test Attendance With the Connection Off

Ask the vendor to show this sequence on a device similar to the ones your teachers use:

1. Sign in and load the required class while connected.
2. Turn the connection off.
3. Mark several pupils present and one absent.
4. Move away from the register and return.
5. Refresh or reopen the application, if the provider says that is supported.
6. Reconnect.
7. Check the register from a second authorised device.

If the offline feature requires the class to be loaded first, staff need to know that. A system can support useful offline work while still requiring preparation before an outage.

Do not treat a page remaining visible as proof that changes have been saved.

Ask what the teacher sees when data is stored locally, waiting to sync, successfully synced or rejected.

## Check What Happens When Two People Change a Record

Suppose a class teacher marks a pupil absent while offline.

Meanwhile, an administrator records that the pupil arrived late from another device.

When the teacher reconnects, which entry remains? Can staff understand what happened?

This is a synchronisation question. The vendor should explain the conflict rule and demonstrate it if more than one person can edit the same information.

Possible approaches include restricting editing, preserving changes for review or applying a defined ordering rule. Ask what the actual product does rather than assuming a particular method is safest for every workflow.

For your school, the important outcome is that a conflict does not silently leave everyone trusting different records.

## Keep Offline Payment Notes Separate From Confirmed Payments

This matters for the bursary.

A parent shows a bank-transfer screenshot while the school has no connection. Staff may record that the parent reported a payment, but the screenshot alone does not confirm that the money arrived.

Ask whether the system distinguishes:

- a reported payment awaiting verification;
- an authorised manual entry;
- a gateway-confirmed transaction;
- a failed or pending online payment.

Never let an “offline-ready” label imply that a new Paystack transaction can be independently confirmed without connectivity.

## Ask About Shared Devices and Local Data

A teacher may use a personal phone. An office computer may be shared by the secretary and bursar.

If records are available offline, ask what remains on the device after sign-out and who can see it. Find out what happens if the device is lost or a member of staff leaves.

Also ask what clears pending data. Browser storage can behave differently from a central database, and clearing site data or resetting a device may affect unsynced work.

The provider should explain its supported behaviour and the precautions staff need to take. Your team should not have to learn those limits by losing a register.

## Offline Access Is Not a Backup Plan

A cached copy on one device does not replace the school’s backup and recovery arrangements.

Ask the vendor how central records are backed up, what can be restored and how the school requests recovery. Ask separately how you can export records.

If the product runs entirely on a local computer, find out who is responsible for making backups and checking that they can be restored.

A file called “backup-final” on the same laptop as the only working database will not help if that laptop fails.

## A Checklist for Your Offline Demo

Take this table to the vendor and fill it in with demonstrated answers:

| Question | Answer to record |
|---|---|
| Which tasks work offline? | Name the tasks individually. |
| What must be loaded first? | Classes, student records or other required data. |
| Which devices and browsers are supported? | Your actual staff devices, not just the demo laptop. |
| How is unsynced work shown? | The status staff should look for. |
| What happens after a refresh or restart? | Whether pending work remains available. |
| How are conflicting edits handled? | The rule and the review process. |
| What needs a connection? | Payments, messages, shared updates and other limitations. |
| What happens on sign-out? | Local-data and pending-change behaviour. |

Ask for the answers in writing if offline use is a deciding factor in your purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can school software work without internet?

Some products support selected tasks offline. Check the tasks, devices, preparation and syncing behaviour during a demonstration.

### Can a new online payment be confirmed offline?

Gateway confirmation requires connectivity. A locally entered payment note should not be confused with a verified gateway transaction.

### Is offline software always a desktop application?

No. Some browser applications support cached records and queued changes. Desktop installation alone does not explain sharing, backup or sync behaviour.

### What should we ask SchoolKit to demonstrate?

Your exact tasks with the connection off, what survives reopening the app and how staff verify that records synced after reconnecting.
