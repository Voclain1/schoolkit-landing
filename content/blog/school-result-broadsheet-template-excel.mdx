## What's in the Template

The workbook has four tabs:

| Tab | What it does |
|---|---|
| **How to use** | Short instructions |
| **Subject scores** | One sheet per subject: 1st test, 2nd test, CA total, exam, total out of 100, grade, remark and subject position for up to 40 students, plus class average, highest and lowest |
| **Broadsheet** | The whole class against 12 subjects: subjects offered, grand total, average, grade and class position, plus subject averages, highest and lowest |
| **Grading scale** | Your grade boundaries and remarks, used by every formula |

White cells are for typing. Green cells calculate themselves — don't type over them.

## How to Use It

### 1. Set your grading scale

Open the **Grading scale** tab. The common scale is already filled in:

| Minimum score | Grade | Remark |
|---|---|---|
| 70 | A | Excellent |
| 60 | B | Very good |
| 50 | C | Credit |
| 45 | D | Pass |
| 40 | E | Fair |
| 0 | F | Fail |

Change the numbers and letters to match your school. Keep the rows in ascending order of minimum score — the formulas depend on it.

### 2. Make one subject sheet per subject

On the **Subject scores** tab, set the maximum for each test and the exam (20, 20 and 60 by default). Then right-click the tab, choose **Duplicate** (or **Move or Copy → Create a copy** in Excel), and rename it for each subject.

Each teacher enters names and scores. The sheet will:

- add the tests into a **CA total**,
- add CA and exam into a **total out of 100**,
- look up the **grade** and **remark**,
- rank the class to give a **subject position**,
- show the **class average, highest and lowest** score.

Scores above the maximum are refused, so a 25 typed into a 20-mark test is caught immediately.

### 3. Fill the broadsheet

On the **Broadsheet** tab, enter each student's subject totals — or link them from the subject sheets so they update automatically. To link, click a broadsheet cell, type `=`, click the student's total on the subject sheet and press Enter.

The broadsheet then calculates each student's:

- **Subjects offered** — blank subjects are not counted,
- **Grand total**,
- **Average** — divided by the subjects the student actually offers,
- **Grade** — from the rounded average,
- **Class position** — ties share a position.

Subject names in the header row are examples; change them to your school's subjects.

## How the Formulas Work

You don't need to understand the formulas to use the template, but if a parent asks how a position was worked out, here is the logic:

- **Total:** `=SUM(CA cells) + exam`
- **Average:** `=SUM(subject totals) / COUNT(subject totals)` — `COUNT` ignores blanks, so a subject a student doesn't take isn't a zero.
- **Grade:** `=LOOKUP(ROUND(average,0), minimum scores, grades)` — rounding first means 69.5 is graded as 70.
- **Position:** `=RANK(average, all averages, 0)` — equal averages get the same rank, and the next rank is skipped.

## Common Broadsheet Mistakes This Template Avoids

1. **Adding raw scores from different maximums.** A test marked out of 50 must be scaled before it's added to a 20-mark CA. Set the maximums on each subject sheet and scale before entry.
2. **Counting a missing subject as zero.** It drags the average down unfairly.
3. **Numbering positions 1, 2, 3, 4 when two students tie.** Tied students share a position; the next student is 4th, not 3rd.
4. **Broken ranges after adding a late student.** The template already covers 40 rows; type into an empty row rather than inserting a new one.
5. **Pasting values over formulas.** If a green cell stops updating, someone typed over it — copy the formula down again from the row above.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a result broadsheet?

A broadsheet is a single sheet that shows every student in a class against every subject: each subject score, the grand total, the average, the grade and the class position. Principals use it to review and approve a class's results before report cards are produced.

### Does the template handle tied positions?

Yes. It uses Excel's RANK function, so students with the same average share a position and the next one is skipped — 1st, 2nd, 2nd, 4th.

### What if a student does not offer every subject?

Leave that subject blank for the student. The template divides by the number of subjects each student actually offers, so a blank is not counted as zero.

### Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets; the formulas work the same way. It also opens in LibreOffice Calc and Excel on Android.

### Can I change the grading scale?

Yes. Edit the Grading scale tab. The common A–F scale is pre-filled, and the tab includes the WAEC A1–F9 boundaries if your school uses them.
