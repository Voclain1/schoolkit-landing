Your nursery and primary school has 86 pupils.

The bursar uses Excel. Teachers prepare report cards in Word. Parents send payment screenshots to whichever member of staff answers first.

You want something better. But the first software demo spends twenty minutes showing features your school may never use.

**What should a small school actually pay for?**

Start with the work you repeat every week. A useful system should make that work easier before it asks you to manage more modules.

## Start With Five Jobs Your School Already Does

For a small Nigerian private school, the first shortlist is usually straightforward:

| Job | What to check in a demo |
|---|---|
| Keep student records | Find a pupil, their class and the correct guardian without searching several files. |
| Track attendance | Mark a class register and review a pupil’s attendance history. |
| Manage fees | See bills, part payments and outstanding balances against the right pupil. |
| Confirm payments | Record a verified payment and produce a receipt. |
| Prepare results | Enter scores and generate the report card your school needs. |

Ask a teacher and the person handling fees to join the demo. They will notice problems that a proprietor watching the dashboard may miss.

A polished chart is useful. A payment screen your bursar understands is more useful on a busy Monday morning.

## Compare the Cost for Your Actual Enrolment

Suppose one provider quotes a flat termly fee and another charges per pupil.

These are **illustrative quotes**, not market prices:

| Quote | Calculation for 86 pupils | Three-term total |
|---|---:|---:|
| ₦40,000 per term | ₦40,000 each term | ₦120,000 |
| ₦500 per pupil per term | 86 × ₦500 = ₦43,000 | ₦129,000 |

The difference is ₦9,000 across three terms, before other charges.

Now ask whether either quote includes onboarding, staff training, report-card setup, parent messages and data export. A lower subscription can become the higher bill if those are all extras.

Also ask what happens at 101 pupils, 151 pupils or 201 pupils. You want to know the next price before you reach the limit.

## Test a Part Payment Before You Choose

Give every vendor the same example:

> A pupil’s bill is ₦95,000. The parent pays ₦60,000 today and ₦35,000 next month. Show us both payments, the balance after the first payment and the final receipt.

After the first payment, the balance should be **₦35,000**.

Then make the example slightly less tidy:

- The parent has two children in different classes.
- One child uses the school bus.
- The parent sends a single bank transfer.
- A member of staff records the payment against the wrong child.

Ask how the software allocates the payment and corrects the mistake while preserving a clear record.

Small schools have these problems too. They just have fewer people available to sort them out.

## Check the Report Card Your Parents Will Receive

Bring a copy of your current report card to the demonstration.

Ask the provider to show:

- your CA and exam weighting;
- subject totals and grades;
- teacher comments;
- attendance information;
- the difference between a standard template and a branded report;
- how approved results reach parents.

If nursery classes use a different assessment format from primary classes, show both.

Do not assume a system that produces a good secondary-school report automatically suits your nursery assessment sheet.

## Plan the Move From Excel

Before importing anything, tidy the student list.

Give each pupil a consistent identifier. Check duplicate names, class assignments and guardian phone numbers. Review opening balances with the accounts officer.

For example, if a pupil owes ₦22,000 from last term, decide whether that becomes an opening balance or a separate arrears charge. Do not add it both ways.

Start with one class and compare the imported records against your source file. Once the figures agree, continue with the rest of the school.

Then agree on a cutover date: the date from which the software becomes the working record. Keep the old file as a reference instead of letting staff maintain two different versions of the balance.

## What Can Wait Until Later?

A small day school may not need boarding management. A school that does not sell uniforms may not need stock control yet.

That is fine.

Make a short list of what you need **this term**, what you expect to need **next session**, and what does not apply to your school.

Ask whether you can add features later without creating a new student database. That matters more than buying everything immediately.

## A Demo Checklist You Can Use

Before paying, ask the vendor to complete these tasks with sample records:

1. Add a pupil and link the right guardian.
2. Generate a class bill.
3. Record two part payments and show the remaining balance.
4. Correct a mistaken entry.
5. Take attendance.
6. Produce a report card using your grading structure.
7. Give a teacher access without exposing unnecessary financial records.
8. Export student and payment records.

Ask who helps if something goes wrong on report-card day, and what support is included in the quoted price.
