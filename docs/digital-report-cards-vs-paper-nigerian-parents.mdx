Most schools that "go digital" with report cards do the simplest possible version of
it: the same paper template, recreated as a PDF, generated from a spreadsheet formula
instead of handwritten. That's a real improvement over paper — no more illegible
handwriting, no more physical distribution logistics — but it's not actually what most
parents mean when they ask a school if results are "online now."

## What parents are actually asking for

When you talk to parents directly, the request is rarely "can I get a PDF instead of
paper." It's usually one of these:

- **"Can I see this as soon as it's ready, not weeks later?"** A PDF that still gets
  distributed once a term, on the school's schedule, doesn't solve the actual
  complaint — it's the same delay in a different file format.
- **"Can I see how my child is doing over time, not just this term?"** A single
  static report card shows one snapshot. Parents are often more interested in the
  trend — is this subject improving or slipping — than the isolated number.
- **"Can I understand what this score actually means?"** A raw percentage or grade
  letter, with no context about class average or what's expected at this level,
  leaves parents guessing at whether a result is a problem or perfectly normal.
- **"Can I get this without asking my child to bring home a piece of paper?"** For
  parents who travel, work irregular hours, or have multiple children in different
  schools, timing results around a physical handoff is its own source of friction.

## Why this matters for how a school chooses a results system

A results platform that just outputs a nicer-looking PDF solves a formatting problem.
A results platform built around what parents are actually asking for solves a
communication problem — which is the harder, more valuable thing to get right:

- **Results should reach parents directly**, not depend on a child remembering to
  hand over a document.
- **Historical results should be visible in one place**, so a parent can see a
  trend across terms without digging through old paperwork.
- **Context should travel with the number** — where a score sits relative to the
  class, not just the number in isolation.

## What to check before adopting a "digital" report card tool

- **Does it notify parents directly, or still rely on the school to distribute
  manually once results are generated?**
- **Can a parent see last term's results next to this term's, or only the current
  one?**
- **Is the underlying computation actually correct for how your school grades** —
  WAEC-aligned computation, correct weighting, correct handling of resits — or is it
  just a template with the right logo?
